# Forecast rationale notes (removed from the model; rewrite in your own words for the report)

## Australian Equities

- Method and drivers: Grinold-Kroner: forward yield less dilution, plus real earnings growth and inflation, with half the P/E gap to its long-run average closing; averaged with cash plus the DMS premium.
- Why it differs from history: Valuations now sit above their long-run average, so part of the re-rating behind the 20-year return is assumed to reverse.

## World Equities

- Method and drivers: Grinold-Kroner in AUD (relative PPP adds Australian rather than foreign inflation); low dividend yield and a P/E above average; averaged with the build-up.
- Why it differs from history: History was lifted by a US re-rating and a falling AUD; PPP implies only a small currency drift from here.

## Emerging Markets

- Method and drivers: Grinold-Kroner with near-average valuation and higher growth, offset by heavier dilution.
- Why it differs from history: Little repricing drag because valuations are close to average.

## Listed Property

- Method and drivers: REIT dividend yield less issuance plus rent growth; build-up uses the data beta to world equities.
- Why it differs from history: Yield-based view replaces a history that included the GFC collapse in REITs.

## Commodities

- Method and drivers: Collateral (US cash) plus a conservative long-run excess return and the PPP drift.
- Why it differs from history: Futures returns depend on the shape of the curve (Erb & Harvey 2006), so 20-year history is a poor guide.

## Direct Property

- Method and drivers: Cap-rate model: gross rent yield less running costs and depreciation (RBA estimates), plus rent growth; averaged with a beta build-up.
- Why it differs from history: Net yield is low after costs, so the forecast sits below the appraisal-smoothed history.

## Hedge Funds

- Method and drivers: Cash plus a 0.35 equity beta times the expected premium; zero net alpha; fees added back to state it gross.
- Why it differs from history: Investor-weighted evidence (Dichev & Yu 2011) argues against extrapolating index alpha.

## Private Equity

- Method and drivers: Cash plus a 1.2 levered beta times the premium, plus a 1% net premium; fees added back.
- Why it differs from history: Recent vintages have earned smaller premiums than the 1984-2008 funds in Harris, Jenkinson & Kaplan (2014).

## Australian Fixed Income

- Method and drivers: Starting yield to maturity less expected credit losses; horizon is close to duration, where yield is the best predictor.
- Why it differs from history: Yields are far above the 20-year average return, which was depressed by the 2020-22 rate rise.

## World Fixed Income

- Method and drivers: Covered interest parity: foreign yields plus the hedge carry from higher AUD cash rates, less a duration and basis adjustment.
- Why it differs from history: Higher global yields and positive AUD hedge carry lift the forecast above history.

## Cash

- Method and drivers: Average of the market-implied path (5-year yield less term premium) and today's cash rate.
- Why it differs from history: Cash rates are well above the 20-year average after the 2022-26 tightening cycle.
